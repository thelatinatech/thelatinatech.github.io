# The Human Behind AI: The Reminder I Needed to Stop Waiting Until I’m Ready


One of my favorite things about technology is that behind every tool, feature, and product is a human story. We often talk about AI through the lens of models, capabilities, benchmarks, and the latest breakthroughs, but sometimes the most interesting part isn’t the technology. It’s the person who decided to build it.

During the session **“The Human Behind AI: Anshita’s Journey from Curiosity to OpenAI,”** I got a reminder that building in tech isn’t just about having the perfect technical background.

It’s about curiosity.

It’s about showing up.

And maybe most importantly: It’s about being willing to start before you feel ready.

---

# The Biggest Growth Wasn’t Technical

One quote from Anshita that really stuck with me:

> “I stopped waiting to feel ready.”

And honestly? That sentence hit harder than any technical concept could. Because I think a lot of us in tech know this feeling.

The feeling of:

“I need to learn just one more thing before I start.”

“I need more experience before I apply.”

“I need to be better before I share my work.”

“I need to know more before I call myself a builder.”

But the reality is that confidence rarely comes first. Confidence comes *after* you take the leap.

Anshita’s journey was a reminder that growth happens through action:

✨ Ask for opportunities before confidence arrives.
✨ Ship something useful before it feels perfect.
✨ Listen closely enough to let people change the product.

The people who create impactful technology aren’t necessarily the people who waited until they knew everything.

They’re the people who started.

---

# Building What Matters Starts With Your Own Life

One of my favorite takeaways from this session was the idea of starting personal.

Before building something massive, look around you. What problems do you experience every day? What annoys you? What could be easier? What do you wish existed?

Anshita shared examples of projects built from everyday moments, like her coworker using AI to transform his camera roll into a digital wardrobe.

Something that starts simple:

📸 Photos from your camera roll
⬇️
👗 AI extracts clothing pieces
⬇️
🗂️ Organized wardrobe gallery
⬇️
✨ New outfit ideas

It’s not just about creating technology for technology’s sake. It’s about noticing small problems and asking: “Could this be better?”

This question is where so many great ideas begin!

---

# AI Makes Experimentation More Accessible

One thing that excites me about this AI era is how much easier experimentation has become. You don’t need a full team. You don’t need years of experience. You don’t need to have everything figured out. You can start with an idea, explore what’s possible, and build from there.

Whether it’s:

🤖 Creating an AI assistant
🎨 Experimenting with creative tools
📚 Building something that helps people learn
🛠️ Automating a personal frustration
📱 Prototyping an app idea

The barrier between “I wish something existed” and “I made something” is getting smaller, and that’s a pretty exciting place to be.

---

# My Biggest Takeaway

This session reminded me that building is not reserved for people who already have all the answers. It’s for people who are curious enough to ask questions, who are willing to experiment, and for those who care enough to make something better.

I’ve spent a lot of my career learning about tech and cyber, but one thing I’m continuing to learn is that being a builder is also a mindset.

It’s choosing curiosity over perfection. Action over waiting. It’s being okay with v1 being messy.

---

# What I’m Taking With Me

A few reminders I’m carrying forward:

💡 **Stop waiting for permission.**
You don’t need to feel 100% ready before trying something new.

💡 **Build from your own experiences.**
The best ideas often come from solving problems you personally understand.

💡 **Share early.**
Feedback is part of the creation process, not a final step.

💡 **Let people shape what you build.**
Listening is just as important as creating.

💡 **Curiosity is a superpower.**
You don’t have to know exactly where you’re going to start exploring.

---

The biggest lesson from this session wasn’t about AI at all - it was about being human, having ideas, following curiosity, trying things, and trusting that growth happens along the way.


So here’s your reminder (and mine):
- Build the thing.
- Write the post.
- Apply for the opportunity.
- Share the idea.
- Start before you feel ready.

Because you never know where curiosity might take you! ✨
