One of my favorite parts about being in tech is that there is *always* something new to learn. The tools change. The platforms evolve. The way we build keeps getting reinvented.


And lately, one phrase keeps popping up everywhere: **Vibe coding.**

Before this session, I’ll admit, I was still trying to understand what vibe coding actually meant beyond the internet buzzword. Was it just “asking AI to write code for you”? Was it replacing developers? Was it another trend that would disappear in a few months?

After attending **“From Idea to Product: Building with Gemini” with Abirami Sukumaran, Staff Developer Advocate at Google**, I walked away with a much clearer perspective:

Vibe coding isn’t about letting AI do everything.

It’s about learning how to become a better builder.

---

## The Future of Building Is About Intent

One quote from presenter and Google Staff Developer Advocate, Abirami Sukumaran, really stuck with me:

> “Vibe coding is not a choice anymore. If you need to be relevant and you need to be fast, use vibe coding. But don’t rely on it entirely... you need to understand the underlying mechanisms too.”

And honestly? This perfectly captures where I think we are headed.

AI is changing the way we create. It’s lowering the barrier between having an idea and actually bringing that idea to life.

You no longer need to spend months learning every technical detail before you can experiment.

You can have an idea on Monday, prototype it on Tuesday, and potentially have something people can interact with by the end of the week.

But the magic isn’t just in telling AI, “build me an app.”

The magic is knowing:

* What problem are you solving?
* Who is this for?
* What does the user experience look like?
* What data does the application need?
* Where should that data live?
* Where does AI actually add value?

The best builders aren’t just writing prompts. They’re thinking like architects.

---

# The Three-Step Framework: Think → Build → Publish

One thing I loved about this session was how simple Google’s approach to building with AI felt.

Instead of getting overwhelmed by all the possibilities, start with three steps:

## 1. Think: Define the Intent

Before touching any tools, start with the idea.

Ask yourself: What problem are you trying to solve?

Maybe it’s:

✨ A productivity tool
✨ A personal portfolio
✨ A habit tracker
✨ An interactive dashboard
✨ A game
✨ A resource library

The goal isn’t to start with technology.

The goal is to start with curiosity.

One of my biggest takeaways was that a strong idea paired with a strong prompt can become a starting point for creation.

---

## 2. Build: Turn the Idea Into Reality

This is where tools like Google AI Studio and Antigravity come in. Instead of manually building every component from scratch, AI can become your coding partner.

You describe your idea.
You explain the experience you want.
You iterate.
You refine.
The AI helps generate the foundation, while you guide the vision.

But one thing that stood out to me: **AI works best when you treat it like a collaborator, not a magic button.**

The better the context you provide, the better the output.

A simple prompt:

> “Help me build an app to track my food and water intake.”

becomes much more powerful when you add:

* Who the app is for
* What the user experience should feel like
* What features matter
* Whether AI should be included
* The visual style you want

The lesson?

Don’t just prompt for output. Prompt with intention!

---

## 3. Publish: Share What You Build

The final step is taking your idea from something sitting on your laptop to something people can actually use. And this part was honestly exciting.

The ability to go from: “I have an idea” to “I built something” to “Here is the link” is such a powerful shift. Technology has always been about creating solutions and now AI is helping more people become creators.

---

# My Biggest Takeaway: AI Won’t Replace Builders but It Will Expand Who Gets To Build

One of my favorite things about this session was realizing that building software is becoming more accessible. You don’t have to fit the traditional image of a developer to create.

Designers can build. Product managers can build. Students can build. Curious people can build. People with ideas can build. And as someone who has spent a lot of my career learning technology from the outside in, that idea is incredibly exciting!

For a long time, I thought the hardest part of building was knowing every programming language, every framework, and every technical detail. But maybe the hardest part has always been something else: having the courage to start.

---

# What I’m Taking With Me

After this session, I want to approach AI differently. Not just as a tool that helps me work faster, but as a tool that helps me experiment, create, and bring ideas to life.

A few things I’m taking away:

💡 **Start with the problem, not the technology.**
The best products solve real problems.

💡 **AI is a collaborator, not a replacement for understanding.**
Use AI to accelerate learning, but continue building your technical foundation.

💡 **Better prompts create better outcomes.**
Context, goals, and constraints matter!

💡 **Iteration is part of the process.**
The first version doesn’t have to be perfect. Build, test, improve, repeat.

💡 **Everyone can be a builder.**
You don’t need permission to create something.

---

Maybe the future of technology isn’t just about who knows the most code.

Maybe it’s about who has the curiosity to ask better questions, the creativity to imagine new possibilities, and the willingness to experiment. 🤖✨
