For a long time, I thought working hard was enough to get recognized. It wasn't until a conversation with my manager that I realized an important career lesson: people can't support what they don't know about. Here's how I learned to build visibility and advocate for myself.


---
> I won’t know what you’re doing unless you tell me.

My team and manager didn’t understand why I was stressed all the time. Little did they know, I was juggling my daily responsibilities of data analysis, editing presentations, chasing people for deliverables, and on top of that I was working on 2 projects outside of my team, volunteering outside of work with two organizations, managing grad school, and chores at home. *Yikes*.

All of this was somehow brought up during a 1-on-1 meeting with my manager and he was shocked. He had no idea. We were able to create a plan together to prioritize my deliverables to make everything more balanced. After the meeting, my manager said the quote above to me. It seemed so simple and intuitive but I had overlooked it, and it’s stuck with me since.

My experience wasn't unique. According to [The Self- Promotion Gap](https://www.selfpromotiongap.com/), the majority of women (about 69%) avoid talking about their strengths and accomplishments. Instead, they would rather minimize their successes than tell others about them.

I had avoided mentioning anything because I didn’t want to sound like I was bragging or complaining, but in doing so I was stalling my career growth and new opportunities by keeping my manager in the dark. Like many early-career professionals, I wanted opportunities to grow through promotions, increased responsibilities, and new projects. But I was unintentionnally limiting those opportunities by keeping my manager in the dark.


Since then, I've heard similar advice from female leaders at Cisco speak on career growth. Doing great work is important, but people also need to understand the impact you're making.

**Get Visibility by Highlighting Your Accomplishments**

As with anything, it takes practice to get used to it and to get good at it. Before you can communicate your value to others, you need to understand it yourself.

# Do a self- analysis

Ask yourself:

- What are my greatest strengths?
- What are my greatest weaknesses or improvement areas?
- When people think of me, what do I want others to associate with me? (aka what does my personal brand look like?)

## Work on your personal brand

- Update your elevator pitch, email signatures, bios, AND

- Make sure your [LinkedIn](/thelatinatech.github.io/_posts/2020-10-09-linkedin-101.md) profile and professional presence reflect the skills, experiences, and goals you want others to associate with you. 

## Communicate Your Impact

- Make it a habit to update a key leader or stakeholder on your progress towards a goal regularly

- Send regular updates and status reports to a distribution list of people who would appreciate being kept up to date on what you’re doing

- Create a presentation on best practices to share with other teams

## Make Yourself Visible

- Contribute to an article, blog post, LinkedIn post, industry publication

- Ask well-thought out questions at a meeting that shows what you know

- Ask for a spot on a meeting agenda to share updates, knowledge, lessons learned

- Start a meeting by asking each individual to share a recent accomplishment, best practice OR give kudos to other people and their accomplishments


Career growth isn't only about doing great work, it's also about making sure others understand the impact of that work. Advocating for yourself isn't bragging; it's giving others the information they need to support your growth.
