Your LinkedIn profile is often your first impression and it only takes a few seconds for someone to decide whether to learn more. Here are three simple profile updates that can make you more memorable and help you stand out to recruiters and new connections.


Networking events come and go, but your LinkedIn profile is often the first impression people have of you. Whether you're meeting recruiters, classmates, or industry professionals, a polished profile can make people much more likely to remember *and* connect with you!

This past week I spent connecting with so many wonderful Latinos at the virtual LTX Fest. We’re mainly forming connections through LinkedIn so es *súper* importante tener un strong profile ready for forming those first impressions and making people want to connect with you.

# Most Viewed on your LinkedIn

These 3 sections of your LinkedIn profile are very powerful in making a good first impression, are easy to update, but can be often overlooked.

## Profile Picture

Your profile photo is one of the first things people notice. A clear, professional-looking photo helps build trust and makes your profile feel more approachable.

Your profile picture should be up to date, have only you in the picture, and reflect what you actually look like. It’s recommended to use a high quality picture taken in good lighting and a non-distracting background.

## Headline

This gives a sneak peek even before clicking into your LinkedIn profile. The headline should give a peek into your profile, who you are, what you do, what your goals are.

    Computer Science Student

    or

    Computer Science Student | Aspiring Cybersecurity Analyst | Passionate about Cloud Security

Which one would you want to connect with?

### Show how you add value

- Use relevant keywords and numbers
    - Top E-commerce Email MarketerSent 100’s of millions of emails resulting in $25+ million”
- Keep it Clear
    - B.S. Computer Science, Seeking internships 2022

## LinkedIn Summary

This is your chance to share your story and showcase your personality. The best summaries that I have come across are written very clear and concise, have a catchy first sentence (aka a hook), are written straightforwardly (no fancy jargon), and in the correct tense (past job experience vs. present job experience).

*240 characters*

is what others will see on your profile Summary before they have to click the “See More…”, so make what you say count.

Think of answering the following questions:

- What problems do you solve? What have you done?
- What are your goals? What are your passions?
- What are your career or education experiences? achievements?
- What else should people know about you? What makes you unique?

Your LinkedIn profile doesn't have to be perfect before you start networking. Small improvements, like updating your photo, writing a stronger headline, or refreshing your summary, can make a big difference in how you're perceived. Spend 15 minutes on these three sections today, and your future self will thank you.
