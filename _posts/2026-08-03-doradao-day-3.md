How to Tell Stories People Don't Forget


One thing I've already noticed about the Girls Who Yap Fellowship is that this first week's sessions have already left me with at least one idea I can't stop thinking about. Today's storytelling masterclass with Renee Lynn Frojo was one of those.

Instead of talking about sotrytelling as something only journalists or content creators do, she reframed it as the way we earn trust, share ideas, and help people connect with what we care about. One line that stuck with me was that a story isn't *just what happened - it's why it matters*. That completely changed how I think about telling my own story.

My favorite takeaway was her simple framework:
> I do (A) for (B) by (C) because of (Y).

It's such a small and simple formula, but it forces you to get clear on waht you do, who you help, how you help them, and, most importantly, WHY. The "because" is where the story lives.

Another idea I loved was thinking about your North Star: the belief or big idea that connects everything you create. It's less about having the perfect niche and more about knowing what you;re trying to change or make people believe.

As someone who's been rebuilding my blog and figuring out what I want to create, this session felt like permission to stop worrying about sounding impressive and start focusing on being memorable. AI can help us write faster, but our perspective is still the one thing no one else can replicate.

Now excuse me while I go tell stories about... well, *everything*. ✨
