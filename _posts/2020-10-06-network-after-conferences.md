Networking doesn't end when the conference does. If you're unsure what to say after meeting someone at an event, this guide will show you how to follow up on LinkedIn with confidence.


After one conference, I realized I'd met more than a dozen incredible people, but by the time I got home, I could barely remember everyone's names. That's when it clicked: the most important part of networking doesn't happen during the conference: it happens afterward. The goal isn't just to collect names; it's to turn brief conversations into lasting professional relationships.

# Follow Up Within 48 Hours

In my experience, the majority of conference attendees use either LinkedIn or Twitter to connect at conferences. For the sake of this blog and the common use of it in the corporate/ tech world, let’s focus on LinkedIn.

Once you're home, look up the people you met on LinkedIn using their name, company, university, or location. After finding their profile, send a connection request with a personalized note.

Most people simply click Connect *without* including a message. A short, personalized note reminds the other person where you met and gives them a reason to accept your request.

You will only have 300 characters to send your message, so be clear and concise! I’d recommend using my template below!

# Send a Personalized Request

LinkedIn connection requests are limited to 300 characters, so keep your message short, personal, and specific.

```
Hi <Name>,

It was great meeting you at <BSides Phoenix> yesterday! I enjoyed our conversation about <cloud security> and wanted to stay connected here on LinkedIn.

Looking forward to keeping in touch!
```
---

### Good reasons to connect include:
- You met at the same conference or meetup.
- You attended the same session.
- You share a university, employer, or professional community.
- You enjoyed one of their talks or presentations.
- You admire their work and want to stay connected.

# Build the Relationship

While you may be excited to connect with someone new, do not ask for a job or a reference right away. Instead, take the time to build a strong connection and get to know them first. If you were meeting IRL, you wouldn’t walk up to a random stranger and ask them for a job. Apply that same logic here. 

How do you build a strong connection? 
Ask to set up a quick 30 minute 1:1 or informational interview so you can have uninterrupted time to ask questions and get to know the other person. If neither of you can commit to that, send a message and start a conversation on how they got started in their field, what projects they are working on, or asking what they would recommend to learn more about a specific topic.

# Stay Connected

Regardless of whether your connection is new to your network, or someone you’ve been connected on LinkedIn for a while, engage with them online! Even a simple reaction or a quick comment on others’ posts or articles can make a difference. It shows that you are actively staying up-to-date with whatever it is they’re doing. This can also be a good sort of icebreaker (aka a good excuse) to reach out to them and reconnect.


Conferences may only last a day or two, but the relationships you build can last throughout your career. A thoughtful follow-up message, genuine curiosity, and consistent engagement can turn a brief introduction into a meaningful professional connection.
