Stephen Bugaj has spent his career bouncing between the early days of VR, Pixar, video games, Hollywood, and now generative AI. Across all of those industries, one thing has stayed the same: technology changes constantly, but great stories don't.


As AI gets better and makes creating easier than ever, it's tempting to think the tools are the magic. But they're not! 

The magic has always been the human behind the keyboard asking:

* What story am I trying to tell?
* How do I want someone to feel?
* Why should anyone care?

The rest is just the medium.

One piece of advice that really stuck with me was to **stop waiting until everything feels polished**. 

Tell stories with your phone. Write things down. Film your friends. Share ideas before they're perfect. Become a storyteller in your everyday life instead of waiting for permission to call yourself one.

I also loved hearing that AI slop isn't just content that's *technically* bad, but that it's content that feels *disconnected*. You can almost feel when something was generated without intention. And I think that's what makes human creativity more valuable than ever.

Whether you're making a film, designing an app, writing a blog post, or building an AI product, the goal isn't to show people what you made. It's to make them feel something.

---

### My biggest takeaways

✨ The fundamentals of storytelling haven't changed in thousands of years. Technology evolves; human emotion doesn't.

✨ AI is a creative amplifier, not a replacement for perspective.

✨ Filmmaking and product design have more in common than I expected. They're both about understanding your audience and managing complexity.

✨ Don't wait to become "a creator." Start telling stories today, exactly where you are.


This session reminded me that no matter how technical my career becomes, I never want to lose sight of the human on the other side of the screen. Because people rarely remember the feature you built, but they'll remember how you made them feel.
