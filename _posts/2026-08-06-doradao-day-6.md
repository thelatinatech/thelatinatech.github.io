In creating an AI film, it turns out the hard part isn't working with AI, it's telling a good story.


When I saw this session about AI filmmaking, I expected to leave with a list of prompts and tools. Instead I left again thinking about storytelling.

Einat Graitser, an AI filmmaker and cinema educator, spent surprisingly little time talking about which AI models to use. Instead, she walked us through the same questions filmmakers have always asked:

- Who is this character? 
- What do they want?
- What's standing in their way?
- What changes by the end?

It was a good reminder that AI can generate cool visuals, but it cannot create a meaningful story for you. You have to be the director.

---

One of my favorite ideas from the session was that **an idea isn't a story yet**. An idea is just a question, and the story begins when the character has to make a choice.

Another line that stuck with me was:
> Generating options is not casting. Choosing is directing.

It's so easy with AI to keep clicking "generate" over and over, hoping that the perfect image will eventually appear. But, the creative work isn't just generating more, it is deciding what deserves to stay.

The same thing applies outside of filmmaking too. Whether you're writing a blog post, building an app, designing a project, or making content, there comes a point where you have to stop asking AI for infinite possibilities and start making creative decisions yourself.

---

I enjoyed how practical the session was. Instead of starting with an entire universe, Einat encouraged us to start with one character, one moment, and one clear change.

It was a helpful remind that you don't have to build your whole world, your whole idea today, you just have to finish one scene. This reminder also tied in with another wise quote Einat dropped: Failure is directional data.

Instead of endlessly tweaking everything when something doesn't work, isolate one problem, try changing one variable, and understand "what exactly failed?".

---

We wrapped up talking about all the tools filmmakers are experimenting with today - Kling, Veo, ElevanLabs, LTX Studio, and more. But Einat's driving point was that regardless of the tools you use, you must give yourself the chance to Dream Big. 

Dream big. Dream bigger. Start before you feel ready. Start even when you don't know how to do something.

Einat shared that she's currently vibe coding an app herself, even without knowing any programming languages... If an AI filmmaker can jump into building software without a programming background, maybe the rest of us don't need to wait until we feel "qualified" either.

---

## My biggest takeaways
✨ AI doesn't replace storytelling, it amplifies it.
✨ A great prompt starts with a great story.
✨ One character. One moment. One change.
✨ Stop generating. Start directing.
✨ Failure isn't a dead end; Failure is feedback pointing you toward the next iteration.

I will say, I came into this fellowship hoping to learn to create apps with AI, but I'm realizing I'm also learning how to become a better creator.
